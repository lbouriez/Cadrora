# Make the site yours after deployment

This is steps 11–12 of the [setup and launch guide](deployment.md#existing-repository-cloudflare-builds). Your site is online, but it still needs your studio's identity and real information before you share it with clients. [The admin guide](admin-guide.md) covers everyday gallery work.

1. **Sign in.** Open `https://your-domain/admin/login` with the `ADMIN_PASSWORD` saved during installation. The password is in the ignored local `.artifacts/setup/admin-credentials.env` file if you have not yet moved it to a password manager. Do not publish this file or put the password into GitHub or Cloudflare settings.
2. **Set the public appearance and text.** In **Site settings → Website**, set your studio name, available languages, default language, descriptions, footer and light/dark appearance. In **Sessions**, choose what you offer and review the title, description, duration, prices and included items in each enabled language. Decide which sessions appear on Home and on the Sessions page. Review About text and its photo too. Save text separately from photo-upload success; use the translation controls to fill missing FR/EN copy.
3. **Add real contact details.** In **Site settings → Contact**, enter your email, phone and studio location. **Where you travel** is a short visitor-facing description such as “Montréal and the South Shore”; it is not another studio address. For the optional map, type a city, choose a suggestion to fill the coordinates, enter your approximate travel distance in kilometres, and save. You can type coordinates manually if city suggestions are unavailable. Leave all three map fields blank to hide the map. City lookup is optional and sends only your search text to a third-party map service; visitors do not use it.
4. **Replace the brand image and favicon in Git.** Your profile's `sites/<site-id>/site.ts` sets `logoUrl`, used by the site header. Its `sites/<site-id>/profile.json` sets `document.icon`, used for the favicon and home-screen icon. Put your own licensed, square PNG files under `public/brand/` and point the fields to their `/brand/<file>.png` URLs. They may share one file, or you may use a transparent header mark and an opaque icon that remains visible on light and dark browser tabs. Commit and deploy for the new images to appear; browsers may cache an old favicon, so test in a fresh tab or private window. The built-in Cadrora and Atelier Giulia profiles are working examples. A logo is not an admin setting: it must be available in the static page before the admin API responds.
5. **Replace sample presentation.** Edit `sites/<site-id>/site.ts` for your FR/EN home-page wording, imagery and section order, and `sites/<site-id>/theme.css` for site-specific colours. Put licensed photos in `public/brand/`. Changes to those files need a Git commit and deployment; gallery photos themselves are uploaded through the admin. The [profile guide](technical/public-website.md#public-profile-configuration) explains what is shared by all sites.
6. **Check before sharing.** Open Home, Services, Galleries, Contact and Privacy on a phone and a computer, in each enabled language and appearance. Verify the logo, favicon, links, contact details and map. Publish a test gallery only after confirming its access and download options. Remove or replace any demo photos and fictional information that are not yours to use.

Contact, language, services, appearance and analytics settings are stored for your own deployment and update without rebuilding. Logo, favicon, home composition and profile imagery are build-time assets; they require a new deployment.

## Launch readiness checklist

Complete this on the deployed hostname, not only in a local preview. Use a small gallery of photos you have permission to share for the test.

- [ ] **Brand and content:** real studio name, logo, favicon, About, sessions and contact details; no unintended sample prices, fictional information or provisional photos. Publish at least one real portfolio collection if the portfolio is part of your launch.
- [ ] **Languages:** read Home, About, Sessions, Portfolio, Contact and Privacy in every enabled language; check only pages you intend to expose. Check page titles and link previews after deploying static profile metadata as well as saving runtime text.
- [ ] **Phone and keyboard:** navigate on a phone and computer; open/close a session detail and photo viewer, reach controls with Tab, and verify contact links.
- [ ] **First client gallery:** create a password-protected gallery, import a small batch, wait until photos are ready, choose its cover and confirm downloads/originals, retention and public-directory inclusion. A listed protected gallery exposes its title/date/description; use an unlisted gallery when those should not appear publicly.
- [ ] **Visitor access:** open the shared link in a private/signed-out browser. Confirm photos are blocked before unlock and appear with the correct password. If downloads are enabled, download a photo and verify the intended format. The owner's automatic access is not a visitor-access test.
- [ ] **Publication and cleanup:** verify the intended published/unlisted state. Take the test gallery offline when finished, or deliberately delete it. Offline retains storage; deletion uses background cleanup. Confirm the deployed scheduled trigger is active and check Maintenance for failed cleanup jobs if space is not reclaimed.
- [ ] **Optional features:** verify face search on a real supported device only if you enable it; otherwise leave it off. Check optional analytics consent/map behavior only if configured. Their absence must not block browsing or contact.
- [ ] **Launch:** complete the relevant domain checks below, resolve any blocking issue, and turn off the site-progress notice when ready. Keep the admin password and your original-photo archive safe; Cadrora is not a replacement for that archive.

If an upload is interrupted, use the existing **Resume** or **Finish unfinished photos** flow in the [admin guide](admin-guide.md#admin-ui). Do not start a second recovery system or manually delete cloud objects to complete this checklist.

## Cloudflare domain checks

- **DNS and canonical hostname:** Confirm the apex hostname has a proxied Worker Custom Domain record and an active edge certificate. For `www`, add a proxied `A` record pointing to Cloudflare's originless placeholder `192.0.2.0`, then deploy a Single Redirect from `https://www.<domain>/*` to `https://<domain>/${1}` with status **301** and **Preserve query string** on. A CNAME alone does not redirect a Worker Custom Domain: the Worker matches exact hostnames. Test both `http://www` and `https://www` with a path and query string. [Cloudflare's Custom Domain and redirect guidance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/configuration/routing/custom-domains/).
- **HTTPS and HSTS:** In **SSL/TLS → Edge Certificates**, confirm the certificate is **Active**, **Always Use HTTPS** is on, the minimum TLS version is **1.2**, and TLS 1.3 is on. Enable HSTS only after trusted HTTPS works; start with **1 month**, with **includeSubDomains** and **preload** off. Check the HTTP redirect and `Strict-Transport-Security` response header. Keep HTTPS active while browsers may retain the HSTS policy. [Cloudflare's HSTS guidance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/ssl/edge-certificates/additional-options/http-strict-transport-security/).
- **DNSSEC:** If Cloudflare is also the registrar, enable DNSSEC in **Domains → Registrations → domain → Settings** and wait for **Active**; **Pending** is not completion. If the registrar is elsewhere, enable it in **DNS → Settings** and publish Cloudflare's DS record at that registrar. Confirm the public DS record and DNS resolution after activation. [Cloudflare's DNSSEC guidance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dns/dnssec/).
- **Traffic protection:** Confirm HTTP DDoS protection is active and inspect the actual **Security → Security rules** list for managed, custom and rate-limiting rules; the zone overview's mention of WAF is not proof that a managed ruleset was deployed. Review authentication rate limiting against the actual API routes. Do not switch on zone-wide Bot Fight Mode without testing login, gallery APIs, media and imports: its challenges can affect API clients and cannot be skipped with a WAF rule. [Cloudflare's Bot Fight Mode limitations](https://developers.cloudflare.com/bots/get-started/bot-fight-mode/).
- **Email and private storage:** Check MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC against the real mail sender and forwarding setup. For domains that send no email, publish `_dmarc` with `v=DMARC1; p=reject;`; an apex `v=spf1 -all` also declares that no server sends for the domain if no forwarding service needs a different SPF record. If the domain also receives no mail, use a single null MX (`0 .`) to prevent mail servers from falling back to its website address. DMARC applies to mail claiming to be from the domain and does not replace inbound Email Routing MX records. Configure an authenticated sender before sending as the domain in the future. Keep the media and model R2 buckets private; only the authorized Worker serves them. [Cloudflare's DMARC guidance](https://developers.cloudflare.com/dmarc-management/security-records/) and [the null MX standard](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7505).
